Your notes are the AE's briefing. The more specific and contextual they are, the better the Discovery call goes — and the better the Discovery call goes, the more pipeline you're credited for closing.

💬 Discovery Questions

Use these to uncover who the real decision maker is — even when the person you're speaking to isn't sure themselves.

"Who on your team ultimately owns decisions around payment processing or AP/AR?"
"If you found a solution that solved [the pain they mentioned], who would need to sign off on it?"
"Beyond yourself, who else would be in the room when a decision like this gets made?"
"Is this something your team could move on independently, or does it need sign-off from finance or the C-suite?"
PILLAR 2

Pain

Pain is the specific operational or financial challenge that's driving the prospect's willingness to consider a change. The more specific you can get — especially in their own words — the more useful this is for the AE going into Discovery.

🔍 Pain Categories & What Good Documentation Looks Like

A strong pain note includes the category and the prospect's specific context. Here are examples of what sufficient documentation looks like:

High processing fees
"Paying ~2.9% on all card transactions; CFO wants to reduce by 40+ bps"
Manual reconciliation
"Reconciling 3,000+ transactions/month manually in Excel; 2 FTEs dedicated to it"
Poor payment acceptance rates
"Decline rates around 8–12%; losing $200K+ in revenue monthly"
Fraud / chargeback issues
"Experiencing 1.5% chargeback rate; at risk of processor review"
Limited payment methods
"Can't accept ACH or international cards; losing enterprise deals because of it"
Integration limitations
"Current solution doesn't connect with NetSuite; team doing manual data entry daily"

💬 Discovery Questions

"What's your biggest frustration with your current payment process?"
"Can you walk me through how reconciliation works today — where does it tend to break down?"
"Have you looked into solving this before? What happened with that?"
"If you could fix one thing about your payments setup tomorrow, what would it be?"
"What does this problem cost you — in time, money, or deals you're not winning?"
PILLAR 3

Timeline

Timeline tells the AE how to pace the deal and where to focus their energy. Always ask directly — never assume based on how the conversation felt. A warm conversation doesn't always mean an active project.

📅 Timeline Stages & What They Mean

Immediate0–30 days
Active evaluation underway. AE prioritizes and targets a demo within 5 business days of handoff.
Short-term1–3 months
Defined initiative, likely current or start of next quarter. AE drives urgency and keeps momentum going.
Mid-term3–6 months
Known project but delayed start. AE qualifies deeper on what trigger would accelerate the timeline.
Long-term6–12 months
Exploratory but engaged. AE nurtures and sets a future touch cadence — still worth documenting.
Exploratory12+ months
No defined project yet. AE takes the call with expectations managed; may return to BDR nurture flow.
No Timeline—
The prospect was unclear or deflected. Worth probing a bit more before advancing — a few more questions can turn this into a real timeline.

💬 Discovery Questions

Use a couple of these to pin down where they actually are, not where you hope they are.

"Is there a specific date or quarter you're targeting to have something new in place?"
"What would need to happen for this to move forward in the next 30–60 days?"
"Are there any external deadlines driving this — contract renewals, audits, system migrations?"
"On a scale of 1–10, how urgent is solving this right now versus 6 months from now?"
"Are you locked into anything on the processing side, or is there some flexibility to make a change?"
"When does your current contract come up — is that even a factor here?"
"Is this more of a 'someday' thing, or is there actual urgency behind it for your team right now?"
STEP-BY-STEP

Creating a Qualified Opportunity

Follow these steps every time you create an Opportunity in Salesforce. Consistency here is what makes the whole system work — for the team and for you.

1
Run the qualification call Use the discovery questions in this playbook to work through the three pillars. Ask directly — don't assume. Even a few good answers are more useful than vague impressions.
2
Complete at least 2 of 3 exit criteria fields Fill in Decision Maker, Pain, and/or Timeline in Salesforce. If you have all three, great. If you have two solid ones, that's the minimum to advance — but always push for the third where you can.

  • ✓

  • ✓
    Conduct Discovery within the SLA for the given timeline tier (e.g. within 5 days for Immediate).
  • ✓
    Use the context you provided — the prospect should not need to repeat themselves.
YOUR NOTES

What Good BDR Exit Notes Look Like

Exit Notes are your chance to hand off the full picture. Think of them as a briefing document for the AE — not a form to fill out. The goal is to help them walk into Discovery already understanding the prospect's world.

📝
Aim for 400+ characters. That's roughly 3–5 sentences of useful context. Notes are reviewed in weekly 1:1s — strong notes are a signal of thorough qualification, not extra work.

✅ What to Include

Trigger event
What's driving the urgency? Contract renewal, audit, system migration, new hire, org change — anything that's making this feel timely.
Their own words
Quotes are gold. If they said something memorable about their problem, write it down verbatim. It helps the AE build instant rapport.
Objections you handled
What did they push back on, and how did you address it? Saves the AE from walking into the same resistance blind.
Competitors mentioned
Are they evaluating anyone else? Even a passing mention of who they've looked at or are currently using is useful context.
Tone & sentiment
Were they excited, cautious, just exploring? The AE needs to calibrate their energy before picking up the phone.
The agreed next step
What exactly did you tell the prospect happens next? The AE must honor this commitment — don't leave it vague.
DISPUTE PROCESS

How Disputes Work

The dispute process is a structured way to handle disagreements about qualification — not a way to avoid difficult opportunities. Most handoffs should never need it.

⚖️ Valid vs. Not Valid Dispute Grounds

✓ Valid Grounds for Dispute
DM field lists a title that doesn't match who the BDR actually spoke to
Documented pain can't be verified or doesn't reflect what the prospect said
Timeline marked 'Immediate' but prospect confirms no active project
Contact is not at the right company or in a relevant role
Prospect explicitly said they weren't interested and the meeting was misrepresented
✕ Not Valid Reasons
"The prospect wasn't very warm" — discovery is the AE's job
"They have a competitor" — competition is normal
"They have a long timeline" — Long-term is valid pipeline
"I don't think they'll close" — the AE's instinct doesn't invalidate a BDR's qualification
"The notes weren't detailed enough" — a coaching note, not a dispute

🔁 Dispute Workflow

1
AE files a dispute Marks the opportunity 'Disputed' in Salesforce with written rationale. Must happen within 48 hours of Opportunity creation.
2
BDR responds You have 24 hours to provide supporting evidence — call notes, a recording, or the contact record. Use this window.
3
Managers review jointly Both the AE Manager and BDR Manager review within 48 hours. The outcome is: Uphold, Overturn, or Escalate.
4
Decision is communicated BDR Manager shares the outcome in Salesforce and via Slack. The decision is final unless escalated.
5
Escalation (if needed) Either party can escalate to VP of Sales within 24 hours of the decision — but only with credible new evidence. No further escalation beyond VP level.

📊 Dispute Outcomes

OutcomeWhat Happens
Dispute upheld — criteria not metOpportunity returns to BDR. Credit is not counted. Coaching session follows.
Dispute upheld — criteria were metAE accepts the opportunity and runs Discovery. AE receives a coaching note on the dispute.
Dispute overturnedOpportunity stays with AE. Your sourcing credit is retained. Both managers document the resolution.
Escalation resolvedVP of Sales makes the final call. Both parties comply. No further appeals.
📌
Patterns of repeated disputes from either side are reviewed in monthly pipeline calibration meetings. The goal is alignment — not arbitration.
YOUR SOURCING CREDIT

Protect Your Credit — Qualify Well

Clawbacks exist to maintain pipeline integrity, not to penalize BDRs who are doing the right thing. If you qualify thoroughly and document honestly, your credit is protected. Full stop.

THE PHILOSOPHY

The goal isn't to take money from BDRs — it's to make sure the pipeline we're all working from is real. A BDR who qualifies honestly will never face a clawback. These provisions are here for edge cases where shortcuts create downstream problems for the whole team.

🛡️ Protections in Place for You

  • ✓
    Clawbacks cannot be initiated more than 30 days after the Discovery call (unless fraud is suspected)
  • ✓
    AEs cannot initiate a clawback unilaterally — both the AE Manager and BDR Manager must agree
  • ✓
    You have 5 business days to respond with counter-evidence before a clawback is finalized
  • ✓
    First-time issues with no prior coaching on record result in a warning, not an immediate clawback
  • ✓
    Partial clawbacks (25–50% of credit) may apply where criteria were met in spirit but documented imprecisely

⚠️ Situations That Can Put Credit at Risk

SituationCredit Impact
Criteria documented don't match what the prospect actually said (call recording contradicts notes) Full clawback
Opportunity returned after a dispute is upheld against the BDR Clawback of credit
Prospect confirms they never agreed to a demo and meeting was not genuine Full clawback
Fields added retroactively after Opportunity creation (Salesforce audit trail) Partial or full — reviewed by managers
AE confirms at Discovery that documented criteria were materially off (with recording) Partial or full — reviewed by managers
MSP courtesy call — prospect confirms they took it out of courtesy with no real commitment or timeline Full clawback
